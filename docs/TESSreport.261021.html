TESS-WDL Dark-Sky Jumps
Field report · Dark Sky Network, southern Arizona · 19 October 2026, updated 21 October 2026

Reading-to-reading jumps in TESS-WDL photometers under dark skies

Emilio Falco, Dark Sky Network (DarkSky Southern Arizona) · 19 October 2026

For the STARS4ALL TESS-W / TESS-WDL developers. Data: three DSN TESS units, one with a co-located SQM, and, since 20 October, three units from the STARS4ALL network for comparison.

Two TESS-WDL units in our network report readings that jump by a median of 0.10–0.20 mag from one 10-minute reading to the next on clear, moonless nights, when the sky itself changes by about 0.01 mag. An older TESS-W (firmware 1.0) in the same network changes by 0.01 mag under the same conditions.

At the one site with a co-located SQM, the TESS-WDL readings are almost never brighter than a fixed floor and fall fainter than it by preferred fractions of the flux. Most dark-sky readings are therefore 0.2–0.4 mag too faint, and the jumps also make clear nights look cloudy to our cloud screening.

Update, 20 October 2026. Three units from the STARS4ALL network, with firmware 1.0, v3.31 and v4.32, do not show the jumps (section 4). One of them, stars1380, runs the same firmware v4.32 as our Arivaca unit, so firmware v4.32 does not cause the jumps by itself.

Update, 21 October 2026: cause identified. The TESS developer explains that a TESS with the datalogger board connected runs in datalogger mode, with or without an SD card, and sleeps between readings. Until now it had not been clear that the problem occurs whenever the datalogger board is in place, even without an SD card. In firmware up to v4.32 the unit then waits at most 10 s for photodiode pulses, which is too short for skies darker than about 20 mag. Firmware v4.331 waits as long as needed. The fault matches every feature of our measurements (section 5).

Selection and method

  • Readings used exactly as reported, one every ~10 minutes.
  • Clear, moonless dark sky: Sun below −18°, Moon below −10°, and our cloud-screening statistic χ² < 0.009.
  • Jump: the absolute magnitude difference between consecutive readings no more than about 12 minutes apart.
  • Tubac comparison: each SQM reading paired with the nearest TESS reading within 3 minutes; each TESS reading used once.

3. Tubac: TESS-WDL against the co-located SQM

  • Floor. There is a sharp lower edge at −0.27 mag and nothing below −0.38. At its brightest the TESS reads 0.27 mag brighter than the SQM, the sign expected from the redder TESS passband. Only 7% of dark readings fall near the floor.
  • Preferred levels. Above the floor the readings cluster at about −0.01, +0.19, +0.37, +0.62 and +0.91 mag, which is 79%, 65%, 55%, 44% and 34% of the floor flux. The peaks stay at the same offsets for SQM values from 21.0 to 21.8, so the losses are fixed fractions of the signal, not a fixed amount of light.
  • Growth with darkness. The median offset is +0.07 mag for SQM 20.5–21.0, +0.19 for 21.0–21.3, +0.35 for 21.3–21.6 and +0.39 for 21.6–22.0.
  • Not weather. The jumps do not correlate with the TESS sky temperature (r = 0.03) and occur on nights that are clear in the SQM record.

Consequences for users

  • Nightly or weekly medians of dark-sky TESS-WDL readings come out 0.2–0.4 mag too faint, because the errors go almost only one way. Taking the brightest reading in each hour reduces the bias but does not remove it.
  • Any cloud screening based on the scatter of the light curve marks clear nights as cloudy. On the Arivaca night in Figure 1, which shows a smooth underlying trend and no extended brightenings, our χ² is 0.08–0.3 for about four hours.
  • Without a co-located reference the problem looks like ordinary noise, so other TESS-WDL owners may not be aware of it.

5. Cause: a 10-second pulse window in datalogger mode

From the TESS developer (21 October 2026): the firmware enters datalogger (DL) mode whenever the datalogger board is connected, whether or not an SD card is present. Until now it had not been clear that the problem occurs with the datalogger board in place, since we never use the SD card. In DL mode the photometer sleeps between readings to save battery; with an SD card it stores the reading there, and without one it sends the reading by Wi-Fi before going back to sleep. Without the board the photometer stays active all the time. The bug, with SD card or Wi-Fi alike, is that after each wake-up the firmware waited at most 10 s for pulses from the photodiode, which is not enough under skies darker than about 20 mag. Version 4.331 waits as long as it needs before sleeping.

Our two TESS-WDL units have the datalogger board connected and no SD card, so they have been running in DL mode. The explanation accounts for what we measured:

  • Fixed reporting interval. Our TESS-WDLs report every ~614 s and the interval cannot be changed: this is the wake–sleep cycle of DL mode. The quiet units, including stars1380 with v4.32, report every 300 s, as expected for units without the board that stay active.
  • Errors in one direction. A pulse count cut short can only lose light. At Tubac the TESS readings are never brighter than a fixed floor (−0.27 mag from the SQM) and fall fainter by varying amounts.
  • Worse under darker skies. Fewer pulses arrive in 10 s as the sky darkens, so the median TESS − SQM offset grows from +0.07 mag (SQM 20.5–21.0) to +0.39 mag (21.6–22.0), and the jumps grow from about 0.11 to 0.23 mag (Figure 2).
  • Clear nights flagged cloudy. The jumps raise the scatter of the light curve, so χ²-based cloud screening marks clear nights as cloudy.
  • No dependence on firmware version alone. stars1380 runs v4.32 like our Arivaca unit but without DL mode, and it is quiet.

Next steps

  • Our units must run on their own batteries and solar charging, so removing the datalogger board is not an option. We will update DSN014-T (Tubac) to v4.331 first and compare it with the co-located SQM over at least two clear nights, then update DSN046-T (Arivaca).
  • v4.331 stays awake longer under dark skies, so we will watch the battery voltage after the update.
  • Past TESS-WDL readings from dark nights are biased faint by a variable amount that cannot be corrected reading by reading. The brightest reading in each hour or night is closest to the true sky; at Tubac this upper envelope sits at a fixed offset from the SQM.
  • Any other TESS running with the datalogger board connected should be updated to v4.331 as well. Without a co-located reference the fault looks like ordinary noise.
